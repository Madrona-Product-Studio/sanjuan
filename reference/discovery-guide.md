# San Juan Islands boat-access exploration guide

The San Juan Islands offer one of the Pacific Northwest's richest cruising grounds, with **seven key islands** spanning everything from walkable harbor towns to wilderness marine parks reachable only by boat. Friday Harbor on San Juan Island provides the most concentrated access to restaurants, galleries, and services directly from the dock, while Sucia Island delivers the archipelago's finest primitive moorage experience across six distinct bays. This guide covers current dining, trails, art, wellness, and moorage logistics for San Juan Island, Orcas Island, Lopez Island, Shaw Island, Sucia Island, Vendovi Island, and James Island State Park — organized for boaters planning multi-day cruises through these waters.

The San Juans reward strategic routing. Most marinas monitor **VHF Channel 78A** (Friday Harbor uses 66A), guest moorage is available at every populated island, and fuel docks operate at six locations across three islands. Peak season runs June through September, when all restaurants, galleries, and transit services operate at full capacity. Off-season visitors will find reduced services but uncrowded moorage and trails.

## San Juan Island: Friday Harbor and Roche Harbor anchor the cruising experience

San Juan Island's two marinas bookend the island and offer fundamentally different experiences. **Friday Harbor Marina** places boaters in a walkable downtown with 100 guest slips, while **Roche Harbor Marina** delivers a self-contained resort village with 250+ guest slips 10 miles to the northwest.

### Friday Harbor Marina and downtown

The **Port of Friday Harbor** operates 500 slips with 100 available for overnight guests, accommodating vessels up to 200 feet. Guest moorage runs approximately **$0.40/foot for day moorage** (May–September), with 30-amp power included. The marina offers fuel (gas and diesel), free pumpout, laundry, showers, a fish cleaning station, and a 4-ton lift crane. Reservations open for summer 2026; check-in is 1 PM with a 14-day maximum stay. Hail on **VHF 66A** when approaching. U.S. Customs operates a dock on the outer breakwater, and Kenmore Air provides seaplane service to Seattle's Lake Union.

Downtown Friday Harbor begins at the dock's edge. **Downriggers Restaurant** sits steps from the marina with waterfront views, serving Pacific Northwest seafood daily from 11 AM to 8:30 PM. **Coho Restaurant**, a block away, has held a Wine Spectator Award of Excellence for over 20 years, offering intimate candlelit four-course tasting menus with farm-to-fork cuisine. **Cask & Schooner Public House** at 1 Front Street North delivers hand-battered fish and chips and craft cocktails in an English pub setting. For morning fuel, **Rocky Bay Cafe** is a two-minute walk. The standout destination requiring transportation is **Duck Soup Inn**, set in a rustic cabin 4 miles north, where nearly all ingredients are sourced or foraged directly from the island — acclaimed by Bon Appétit and Fodor's across its 40+ year run.

Galleries cluster within a three-block walk of the marina. **Arctic Raven Gallery** at 130 First Street S (0.1 miles from the dock) specializes in Northwest Coast Native art — masks, carvings, and prints from Coast Salish, Haida, and Tlingit nations. **WaterWorks Gallery** has represented contemporary Pacific Northwest artists since 1985. **Island Studios** at 270 Spring Street exclusively represents artists living in the San Juans. The **San Juan Islands Museum of Art** at 540 Spring Street (0.3 miles) rotates exhibitions Friday through Monday, 11 AM–5 PM, and administers the **San Juan Islands Sculpture Park** near Roche Harbor — a free 20-acre outdoor park with 150 sculptures across five trails, open dawn to dusk year-round.

For yoga and wellness, **The Studio SJI** at 278 A Street (0.3 miles from the marina) offers drop-in Pilates, yoga, barre, TRX, and sound healing classes Monday through Friday. **Island Soul Studios** at 326 Carter Avenue provides Hatha and Vinyasa yoga in a far-infrared-heated studio with classes ranging from free/donation to $15. **San Juan Island Fitness** at 435 Argyle Avenue adds pool access alongside Monday and Wednesday yoga classes.

Bike rentals sit within two blocks of the marina. **Meat Machine Cycles** at 22 Web Street rents Cannondale e-bikes at **$65–$70/day** and Salsa electric gravel bikes at $100/day, operating April through September. **Discovery Adventure Tours** on Spring Street offers Trek e-bikes with Bosch mid-drive motors and guided tour options. **Cycle San Juan** specializes in multi-day supported touring packages. All shops include helmets, locks, and route guidance; bikes can ride the ferry to other islands.

### Roche Harbor Resort Marina

Roche Harbor's **377-slip marina** offers 250+ guest slips accommodating vessels up to 150 feet. Summer rates (May–September 2026) run **$3.10/foot/night** for boats under 55 feet and $4.20/foot for larger vessels, with 30-amp power complimentary. Spring specials include stay-3-get-1-free promotions. Customs clearance uses the **CBP ROAM app** at an unstaffed booth on G Dock. The marina provides fuel, pumpout, laundry, showers, Wi-Fi, and a well-stocked general store.

Dining is steps from the dock. **McMillin's Dining Room**, in the former home of Roche Harbor's founder, serves 60-day aged prime rib and Wine Spectator–recognized wines Thursday through Monday, 4–9 PM. **Madrona Bar & Grill** offers the island's only sunset-over-water dining on a deck built directly over the harbor (summer seasonal). **Lime Kiln Café** on the wharf is the morning stop for fresh doughnuts and hearty breakfasts. One mile away, **Westcott Bay Shellfish Co.** lets visitors eat oysters where they grow, at beachside picnic tables overlooking cultivated tidelands (May–October, reservation-based 1.5-hour seatings).

The resort complex includes an outdoor pool, Afterglow Spa, tennis, pickleball, an off-leash dog park, and the nightly **Colors Ceremony** (flag retirement with cannon blast, May 10–September 29). The **McMillin Mausoleum** ("Afterglow Vista") is a short walk along the airstrip.

### San Juan Island hiking trails

The island's trail network spans **23+ miles** across three main areas, all free and open year-round (Discover Pass required for Lime Kiln Point parking).

**American Camp** (6 miles from Friday Harbor) anchors the south end with the **Mount Finlayson Loop** — a 3.5- to 5.1-mile moderate hike climbing to 295 feet with views of Cattle Point Lighthouse, Mount Baker, and the Olympic Mountains. The easy **Jakle's Lagoon Nature Trail** (1-mile loop) is the island's most accessible path. South Beach offers 2 miles of ocean bluff walking with possible whale sightings.

**English Camp** (9 miles from Friday Harbor, 3 miles from Roche Harbor) features **Young Hill Trail**, a 2-mile round-trip moderate-to-strenuous climb gaining 650 feet to a summit with **360-degree panoramic views** of the Olympic Peninsula, Haro Strait, and Canadian Gulf Islands. The trail winds through Washington's only native Garry Oak forest and passes an 1860s Royal Marines cemetery.

**Lime Kiln Point State Park** (8.5 miles from Friday Harbor) is one of the world's best **land-based whale watching locations**, centered on an 1919 lighthouse with a 1.4-mile loop trail, restored 1860s lime kilns, and a hydrophone station for listening to live orca calls. The interpretive center operates Memorial Day through Labor Day.

## Orcas Island: three marinas serve the archipelago's most dramatic terrain

Orcas Island's horseshoe shape concentrates attractions along a corridor from Deer Harbor in the southwest to Moran State Park in the east, with Eastsound village as the commercial and cultural hub. Three marinas serve boaters, each with distinct advantages.

### Marina access and getting around

**Deer Harbor Marina** on the southwest shore offers **110 transient slips** with side ties to 120 feet, a fuel dock, grocery store/deli at the dock, heated pool (Memorial Day–Labor Day), and complimentary shuttle service. Kenmore Air seaplanes land directly at the dock. Hail on VHF 78A. Deer Harbor sits 20 minutes by car from Eastsound and 30 minutes from Moran State Park — e-bike or shuttle access is essential.

**West Sound Marina** is the island's largest at 180 slips with 400 feet of guest moorage, the county's most complete chandlery, and haul-out capacity to 30 tons. It functions primarily as a working marina — excellent for provisioning and repairs but limited for on-site dining. Located 10 minutes from the ferry terminal and 12 minutes from Eastsound.

**Rosario Resort Marina** in Cascade Bay places boaters closest to Moran State Park (2 miles). The rebuilt aluminum docks provide 30+ slips and 8 mooring buoys with fuel, shore power, and dinghy dock access ($25 landing fee). Marina guests receive spa access for approximately $15/day. **Important caveat**: Rosario Resort underwent an ownership change in early 2026, and the Mansion Restaurant and spa may be temporarily closed for renovation — confirm status before arrival.

Orcas Island's significant hills make **e-bikes strongly recommended** for any marina-to-attraction cycling. **Wildlife Cycles** in Eastsound and **Orcas Wild** (with pickup at Rosario) rent e-bikes and mopeds. **Orcas Island Shuttle** operates rental cars delivered to any location year-round, plus seasonal bus service. **Orcas Island Taxi** runs 24 hours at approximately $30 per average trip.

### Dining from dockside to James Beard–nominated kitchens

Deer Harbor offers immediate dockside dining at the **Marina Dock Store** (espresso, burgers, fish and chips, pizza) and **Island Pie** (wood-fired pizza, short walk). The **Deer Harbor Inn** property, a 10-minute walk uphill, operates Matthew's Smokehouse with BBQ and smoked meats.

Eastsound village (10–20 minutes from marinas by car) concentrates the island's finest dining. **Matia Kitchen & Bar** earned a **James Beard Award nomination in 2024** for its hyper-local seasonal tasting menus — reservations are essential. **Houlme** carries seven James Beard nominations, serving creative sourdough pizza and seafood small plates (closed Monday/Tuesday). **New Leaf Café** inside the historic Outlook Inn delivers water-view dining with crab cakes and wild salmon. For casual stops, **Brown Bear Baking** is the morning essential (artisanal bread, almond croissants, espresso with bay views), and **Mijitas Mexican Kitchen** serves carnitas tacos and margaritas on an outdoor patio.

Near Rosario, the **Cascade Bay Grill & Store** operates seasonally at the marina with pizza, burgers, and waterside outdoor dining. **Buck Bay Shellfish Farm** in Olga (2.5 miles from Rosario) serves ultra-fresh shucked oysters at picnic tables on a working farm.

### Moran State Park: 5,252 acres and the islands' highest peak

**Moran State Park** contains **38+ miles of trails**, five freshwater lakes, and Mount Constitution at **2,409 feet** — the highest point in the San Juan Islands. Discover Pass required for parking.

The **Cascade Falls Trail** (0.25 miles, easy) delivers a spectacular waterfall in minutes from the trailhead off Mount Constitution Road. The **Cascade Lake Loop** (2.7 miles, easy) circles the main lake with swimming access. The **Mountain Lake Loop** (3.9 miles, easy/moderate) offers a more peaceful lakeside circuit.

For summit seekers, the **Cold Springs Trail** (4.3 miles one-way, difficult, 2,058 feet elevation gain) climbs Mount Constitution's west face with numerous switchbacks and East Sound vistas. The **Mount Constitution Loop** (6.7 miles, difficult, 1,490 feet gain) ascends the steep eastern face from Mountain Lake. At the summit, a **CCC-built stone observation tower from 1936** provides 360-degree views spanning Mount Baker, the North Cascades, Vancouver Island, and Mount Rainier. The summit is also accessible by car.

Beyond Moran, **Turtleback Mountain Preserve** on the west side (15 minutes from Deer Harbor, 10 from West Sound) offers moderate trails with views of West Sound, managed by the San Juan County Land Bank. **Obstruction Pass State Park** at the island's southeastern tip hides old-growth forest trails leading to a secluded beach.

### Art and wellness on Orcas

Eastsound hosts the island's gallery district. **Orcas Island Pottery**, established in 1945, is the oldest pottery in the Pacific Northwest — visitors explore whimsical gardens and watch working artists. The **Orcas Island Artworks** cooperative in Olga (3 miles from Rosario) occupies a 1938 strawberry barreling plant with 45 island artists showing pottery, glass, wood, and fiber work. **Crow Valley Gallery** has operated waterfront since 1959. The annual **Artists' Studio Tour in August** opens private studios island-wide.

**Orcas Mandala Yoga & Bodywork Studio** in Eastsound offers drop-in classes in multiple styles. **Doe Bay Resort & Retreat** on the southeast tip provides outdoor oceanview hot tubs, sauna, cold plunge, and an organic farm café — multiple yoga retreat operators use the venue year-round. Rosario Resort historically offers an indoor therapeutic pool, sauna, and full spa services (confirm current availability due to ownership transition).

## Lopez Island: the friendly isle, flat roads, and Fisherman Bay moorage

Lopez Island is the flattest of the ferry-served San Juans and the archipelago's premier cycling destination — featured by National Geographic Traveler and Bicycling Magazine. Its character is quieter and more agricultural than San Juan or Orcas.

### Moorage in Fisherman Bay

**Lopez Islander Resort Marina** at 2864 Fisherman Bay Road offers **64 slips** with water, 30/50-amp power, fuel dock, fish cleaning station, and crab-boiling platform. The on-site Islander Bar & Grill, Tiki Lounge, pool, hot tub, and convenience store make it a self-contained stop. Free shuttle service runs to the ferry landing. **Caution: only 5 feet of water in the channel at zero tide** — time arrivals accordingly. Hail on VHF 78A.

**Islands Marine Center**, adjacent in Fisherman Bay, provides 100 slips with 13 dedicated transient berths and 700 feet of breakwater. It functions as a full-service boatyard with haul-outs to 50 tons — ideal for repairs and provisioning rather than leisure moorage.

**Spencer Spit State Park** on the northeast shore maintains **11 mooring buoys** (8 north of the spit, 3 south) at $15/night, first-come first-served with a 3-night maximum. Phone registration at the buoys accepts credit cards. The park offers beach landing for kayaks, hiking, camping, and vault toilets.

### Lopez Village dining and provisions

**Lopez Village** sits approximately 1 mile from the resort marina — a pleasant 20-minute waterfront walk or quick bike ride. **HAVEN Kitchen & Bar** is the newest addition, offering upscale Pacific Northwest cuisine with waterfront views. **Ursa Minor** delivers creative agrarian fine dining with local seasonal ingredients. **Setsunai** brings Asian-inspired dishes with revolving seasonal menus. The **Islander Bar & Grill** at the resort marina serves breakfast through dinner with legendary Tiki Bar happy hours and live summer music on the waterfront deck.

For casual fare, **Blue Heron Bistro** offers scratch-made comfort food on a large outdoor patio overlooking Fisherman Bay (primarily summer season). **APIZZAPIE** fires Neapolitan-style pizza in a 700-degree stone hearth (Monday–Tuesday, Thursday–Sunday, 11 AM–7 PM). **Holly B's Bakery** (closed late November–early April) is famous for cardamom coco knots and baguettes. **Blossom Grocery** at 295 Village Road stocks local and organic products.

### Lopez trails: coastal drama on the south end

**Shark Reef Sanctuary** (4 miles from the marina) packs maximum impact into a 1-mile round trip — a boardwalk over wetlands leads through old-growth forest to rocky shoreline with views of Cattle Pass, seal and sea lion colonies, and some of the strongest currents in the San Juans. **Iceberg Point** (8 miles south, within the San Juan Islands National Monument) is the island's top-rated hike: a 2-mile round trip through forest to windswept coastal meadow ending at rocky cliffs, with historic camas lily fields and whale watching opportunities.

**Watmough Bay** (8 miles from the marina) offers a flat 1-mile coastal trail to a sandy cove framed by sheer rock — with Mount Baker perfectly framed between the cove borders on clear days. An advanced scramble up Watmough Head adds extreme elevation and panoramic summit views. The **Spencer Spit State Park** trail system covers approximately 2 miles through forest and along the sand spit, recently expanded by a 0.75-mile Land Bank preserve trail through old-growth cedar and Douglas fir.

### Art, wellness, and cycling

**Chimera Gallery** in Lopez Village (est. 1987) is a cooperative featuring painting, ceramics, glass, fiber, and metalwork. The **Lopez Artist Guild** organizes the annual **Artists' Studio Tour on Labor Day weekend**, opening private studios across the island. **ALTAR Movement Studio** offers yin yoga, ashtanga flow, and meditation with high local ratings. **Lopez Fit** provides drop-in yoga classes Monday/Wednesday/Friday at $15 per session.

Bike rentals are everywhere. **Village Cycles** in Lopez Village (1 mile from the marina) offers e-bikes, hybrids, and performance bikes with $5 delivery anywhere on-island. **Edenwild Island Adventures** stations Trek and Kona bikes at both the village and ferry landing at $39.99/day. The Lopez Islander Resort also rents bikes and kayaks to guests. A full island loop covers **31.5 miles** on quiet, mostly flat country roads.

## Shaw Island: the quietest ferry stop demands self-sufficiency

Shaw Island, the smallest of the four ferry-served islands at **7.7 square miles with 188 residents**, operates essentially without commercial tourism infrastructure by community design. There are **no restaurants, no hotels, no galleries, and no bike rentals**. The **Shaw General Store** at the ferry landing — the island's only commercial establishment — opens seasonally with limited groceries and supplies.

**Shaw Island County Park** at Indian Cove (2 miles from the ferry) provides the island's sole visitor facilities: **11 wooded waterfront campsites**, vault toilets, drinking water (April–October only), a picnic area, sandy beach, and boat launch. No showers exist anywhere on the island. Camping reservations are recommended at (360) 378-8420.

There are no formal hiking trails. The island's **11.5 miles of paved roads and 2.4 miles of gravel roads** serve as walking and biking routes with minimal car traffic — the island can be circled by bike in an afternoon. Shaw's character rewards visitors seeking enforced simplicity: no commercial distractions, just farms, forest, and the historic Little Red Schoolhouse (est. 1891, Washington's longest-running school). Day-tripping by ferry from Orcas (10 minutes) or Lopez is the most practical approach.

## Sucia Island: the crown jewel of San Juan marine parks

Sucia Island State Park — **564 acres accessible only by boat**, 2.5 miles north of Orcas Island — is widely considered the finest marine state park in Washington. Its hand-shaped form creates six distinct moorage bays connected by **approximately 10 miles of hiking trails** through forest, along sandstone bluffs, and past 75-million-year-old fossil beds. The park maintains **48 mooring buoys, 800 feet of linear moorage, and 640 feet of dock space** across all bays. There are no services on the island — no stores, no garbage collection, no reliable cell coverage. Pack everything in and out.

### The six moorage bays

**Fossil Bay** is the most popular anchorage, with **16 mooring buoys and 2 docks** (Dock 2 removed October–April). Named for abundant marine fossils including Washington State's only dinosaur fossil (a theropod femur discovered in 2012), the bay features geological interpretive displays and potable water from early April through September. Watch for shallow water near the dock at low tide.

**Echo Bay**, the largest and deepest bay, holds **14 mooring buoys and 2 linear moorage systems** with room for dozens of additional anchored boats on its flat, sticky clay bottom. This is the primary hub for island exploration — a narrow isthmus separates Echo Bay from Shallow Bay, and the trail system radiates from this crossing point. Potable water runs May through September. Echo Bay is most exposed to southeast winds and fills completely on summer weekends.

**Shallow Bay** faces west with **8 mooring buoys** and room for 40–50 small craft at anchor, averaging only 8–12 feet deep at center. It offers the island's best beaches — sandy shores on three sides — and spectacular sunsets. The northeast shoreline features the celebrated **China Caves**, carved sandstone overhangs and cavities formed by millennia of salt crystal weathering, historically named for their alleged use sheltering smuggled Chinese laborers in the 1800s. A group camping area with covered shelter and composting toilets sits at Pebble Beach.

**Ewing Cove** (4 buoys) is the most secluded moorage, formed between Ewing Island and the Cluster Islands. The gravel beach is excellent for sunbathing, and the Cluster Islands harbor fascinating tide pools. **Anchoring is not recommended** due to swift currents through the slot between Ewing and Sucia. An uncharted submerged rock east of the charted "Fish Haven" damages propellers — use extreme caution entering from Echo Bay.

**Fox Cove** (4 buoys) on the southwest side features dramatic 100-foot sculptured sandstone cliffs and a famous **8-foot mushroom rock** formed by differential weathering. The shallow sandy beach at the head provides dinghy access, and campsites between Fox Cove and Fossil Bay offer the quietest evenings with amazing sunsets.

**Snoring Bay** (2 buoys) is the smallest anchorage, east of Fossil Bay around Wiggins Head, with room for only a couple of additional boats beyond the buoys. Legend holds it was named after a park ranger was caught napping by visiting officials.

### Trails, formations, and camping

The **Sucia Island Loop** (4.2 miles, moderate, 419 feet gain) provides the best overview, covering Johnson Point and Shallow Bay with views of the Strait of Georgia. The **Echo Bay Loop** extends to 8.9 miles for a near-complete island perimeter circuit. The **Ev Henry Trail** from Fossil Bay overlooks Fox Cove and Little Sucia Island, named for the yachtsman who led the island's preservation. Short connector trails link all bays — the **Isthmus Trail** between Echo and Shallow bays is the central junction.

The island's geology is extraordinary. Two rock formations — the **Nanaimo Group** (75-million-year-old marine sediments) and the **Chuckanut Formation** (50-million-year-old river deposits) — are separated by a fault and folded into a visible syncline. Honeycomb weathering patterns cover shoreline rocks throughout. **All fossil collecting is strictly prohibited.**

The park maintains **60 standard campsites plus 4 reservable group camps** distributed across Fossil Bay, Echo Bay, Fox Cove, Ewing Cove, and Snoring Bay areas. Standard sites are first-come, first-served. Facilities include pit and composting toilets, five picnic shelters, and potable water at three locations (seasonal). Bring firewood — nothing is available on the island.

## Vendovi Island: a fragile preserve with a closing access window

**Vendovi Island**, a 217-acre nature preserve managed by the San Juan Preservation Trust, sits between Guemes and Lummi Islands near Bellingham Bay. Purchased in 2010 for **$6.4 million** to prevent development, it opened to the public with strict day-use rules and approximately 2,500 annual visitors.

Access runs **April 1 through September 30, Thursday through Monday, 10 AM–6 PM** (closed Tuesday/Wednesday). All visitors over 18 must register online or at the on-site kiosk. Day use only — no overnight camping, fires, or drones. Groups larger than 10 require advance arrangements at vendovi@sjpt.org.

A **70-foot dock in North Cove** provides side-tie moorage on a first-come basis with a **3-hour time limit**. Kayaks may beach inside the protected cove. **Critical note**: the North Cove breakwater requires an estimated $660,000 in repairs, and SJPT is actively fundraising. Without repair, safe public access could cease — verify current status before planning a visit.

The island offers approximately **3 miles of interconnecting trails** (easy to moderate), including the 1.8-mile Paintbrush Point Trail through dense forest to an overlook, and paths to **6 pristine beaches** including shell-littered Sunset Beach and pebble-lined Sunrise Beach. The absence of deer and rabbits allows exceptionally flourishing native flora — spring brings thousands of camas, paintbrush, and fawn lilies across rocky prairie slopes. Harbor seals use the island for resting and pupping (maintain distance). Water taxi access is available from Anacortes, Bellingham, or Orcas Island via **Island Express Charters** or **Outer Island Excursions**.

## James Island State Park: a Rosario Strait crossing with dramatic rewards

James Island's **581 acres** form an hourglass shape — two forested hills rising 200+ feet connected by a low isthmus with two white sand beaches — located 3 miles west of Anacortes and 0.5 miles east of Decatur Island. The park's remote character and Rosario Strait crossing keep crowds manageable.

### Dock and moorage

**West Cove** is the primary boater access, with a **128-foot permanent moorage float** (256 linear feet, year-round). The cove faces Decatur Island and offers more protection than the east side. The bottom is rocky and steeply sloped — **not a good anchorage site**. A composting toilet serves this area.

**East Cove** holds **4 mooring buoys** at $15/night (year-round, first-come first-served, phone registration available at 833-927-1222). East Cove is exposed to ferry and commercial vessel wakes from Rosario Strait — expect rolling. No dock on the east side.

**Approach considerations**: the nearest launch is Washington Park/Skyline Marina in Anacortes (~8 nautical miles). Crossing Rosario Strait involves **potential 3–4 knot currents**, turbulence, breaking waves, and heavy ferry traffic. Strong current also runs at the West Cove dock. Check NOAA chart 18429 and time crossings with favorable tides.

### Trails, camping, and beaches

The island's **1.5 miles of loop trails** circle the central and southwest portions, starting and ending at the Saddle Area between the two coves. High bluff trails deliver extraordinary views of the San Juan Islands and Thatcher Pass — excellent for bird and whale watching. **The northern half of the island is closed** as a Natural Forest Area. Fall hazards exist along cliff-top sections; surrounding waters are deep and swift.

**13 primitive campsites** spread across three areas: East Cove (sites 1–4), the Saddle Area (sites 5–10, with picnic shelter), and West Cove water trail sites (sites 11–13, reserved exclusively for human-powered or wind-powered watercraft users). All sites are first-come, first-served at $12/night. **No potable water** exists on the island — bring everything. Two white sand isthmus beaches provide excellent kayak landing, beachcombing, and tidepooling.

## Cross-island logistics: connecting the dots by water and wheel

### Water taxis bridge the gaps

**Island Express Charters** from Skyline Marina in Anacortes operates the most extensive water taxi network. One-way adult fares run $45 to nearby islands (Blakely, Decatur), **$79 to Friday Harbor**, $80 to Deer Harbor, and **$112 to Sucia Island** (4-person minimums apply on longer routes). They also deliver groceries to outer islands for approximately $30. **Outer Island Excursions** from Orcas Island runs 11 boats with daily ferry service to Sucia including kayak rentals, and holds the county contract for emergency inter-island passenger service when Washington State Ferries experiences disruptions. **San Juan Safaris** from Friday Harbor offers charter water taxi at roughly $250–$750/hour depending on vessel size.

### Island transit without a car

**San Juan Transit** operates seasonal bus service (mid-May through mid-September) from the Friday Harbor ferry terminal to Roche Harbor, Lime Kiln State Park, American Camp, English Camp, and the Sculpture Park. Day passes cost **$20 adults/$15 kids**, with a bus stop at Cannery Landing adjacent to the marina. On Orcas, **Orcas Island Shuttle** provides rental cars delivered to marinas year-round plus seasonal bus service. On Lopez, the **Lopez Islander Resort** runs a free shuttle van to the ferry for guests. **No Uber or Lyft operates** anywhere in the San Juan Islands.

### Washington State Ferries for island-hopping

Walk-on passengers and bicycles ride **free on all inter-island ferry sailings** (Lopez ↔ Shaw ↔ Orcas ↔ Friday Harbor). Off-peak schedules include approximately 5 daily inter-island trips on weekends. The Sidney, BC international route remains suspended through at least spring 2030. Vehicle reservations for westbound Anacortes departures release in phases (2 months, 2 weeks, and 2 days before sailing) and are strongly recommended in summer.

### Fuel, pumpout, and customs

Fuel docks operate at Friday Harbor, Roche Harbor, Deer Harbor, Rosario, West Sound, and Lopez Islander Resort — all offering gas and diesel. Free pumpout is available at Friday Harbor (including a pumpout boat) and through the Port of Lopez in Fisherman Bay (hail VHF 78). For customs clearance returning from Canadian waters, the **CBP ROAM app** is now the standard method — create a trip before departing, complete a video interview, and receive clearance before anyone boards or disembarks. Friday Harbor maintains a staffed customs dock; Roche Harbor uses an unstaffed ROAM booth. Vessels 30+ feet require a CBP User Fee Decal.

## Conclusion: routing strategy for a multi-day cruise

The most efficient cruising loop starts at **Friday Harbor** for provisioning, downtown dining, and gallery browsing, then heads northwest to **Roche Harbor** for resort amenities, the Sculpture Park, and English Camp access. From Roche Harbor, cross to **Sucia Island** for 1–2 nights of primitive camping across its six bays — Fossil Bay for geology, Shallow Bay for beaches and China Caves, Echo Bay for trail access. Return south to **Orcas Island** via Deer Harbor or Rosario, using a rental car or shuttle to reach Eastsound's James Beard–caliber dining and Moran State Park's summit trails. Cross to **Lopez Island** for the archipelago's best cycling and south-end coastal hikes at Shark Reef and Iceberg Point. **James Island** works as a Rosario Strait stopover when routing to or from Anacortes, while **Vendovi Island** requires a separate day trip from Bellingham or Anacortes given its Thursday–Monday, April–September access window and 3-hour dock limit.

Shaw Island rewards a half-day detour for boaters seeking absolute quiet, while the state marine parks — Sucia, James, and Spencer Spit — represent the soul of San Juan cruising: wilderness moorage where the loudest sound is an anchor chain at slack tide. Plan fuel stops no more than two islands apart, carry paper chart NOAA 18421, and respect the **1,000-yard buffer** required around Southern Resident killer whales. Summer moorage at popular bays fills by early afternoon on weekends — arrive before noon or plan for midweek exploration.
