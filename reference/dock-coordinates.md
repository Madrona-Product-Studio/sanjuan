# Precise Marine Dock Coordinates: San Juan Islands Region

**Comprehensive research using authoritative marine navigation sources has identified exact GPS coordinates and specifications for major marine facilities across the San Juan Islands region.** This data comes from official Washington State Parks databases, NOAA navigation resources, port authority documentation, and verified marine navigation guides. Key findings include precise coordinates for six distinct moorage areas at Sucia Island, detailed specifications for Matia Island's single facility, and identification of 30+ additional public marine facilities across the broader region.

## Sucia Island State Park marine facilities

**Sucia Island State Park operates the region's most comprehensive marine facility network with six distinct moorage areas totaling 48 mooring buoys and over 1,400 linear feet of dock space.**

### Echo Bay (primary anchorage)
- **GPS Coordinates:** 48.7633°N, -122.9102°W
- **Location:** Southeast-facing bay, center of island
- **Facilities:** 14 mooring buoys plus 2 linear moorage systems
- **Specifications:** 800 linear feet total moorage capacity
- **Managing Authority:** Washington State Parks and Recreation Commission

### Fossil Bay (main dock facilities)
- **GPS Coordinates:** 48.7501055°N, -122.9004643°W  
- **Location:** Southeast-facing inlet
- **Facilities:** 2-dock system totaling 640 linear feet
- **Operational Notes:** Dock 2 removed October-April for storm protection
- **Managing Authority:** Washington State Parks and Recreation Commission

### Shallow Bay
- **GPS Coordinates:** 48.7622°N, -122.9172°W
- **Location:** West side of island
- **Facilities:** 8 mooring buoys, 12-foot average depth
- **Characteristics:** Sandy bottom, protected from all but westerly winds
- **Capacity:** 40-50 small craft anchoring space

### Ewing Cove  
- **GPS Coordinates:** 48.7651058°N, -122.8871307°W
- **Location:** Northeast end between Sucia and Ewing Island
- **Facilities:** 4 mooring buoys
- **Navigation Notes:** Swift currents through entrance, anchoring not recommended

### Fox Cove
- **GPS Coordinates:** 48.7527°N, -122.9133°W
- **Location:** Southwest side, bordered by Ev Henry Point and Little Sucia Island
- **Facilities:** 4 mooring buoys
- **Features:** 100-foot sculptured sandstone cliffs, unique 8-foot mushroom rock formation

### Snoring Bay
- **GPS Coordinates:** 48.7488°N, -122.8908°W
- **Location:** Southern portion of island
- **Facilities:** 2 mooring buoys
- **Characteristics:** More secluded location, fewer visitors

## Matia Island State Park marine facilities

**Matia Island State Park maintains a single marine facility at Rolfe Cove, with the remainder of the 145-acre island designated as National Wildlife Refuge.**

### Rolfe Cove (only public facility)
- **GPS Coordinates:** *Precise coordinates to 4+ decimal places not available in public sources*
- **Location:** Northwest side of Matia Island, only permitted landing area
- **Dock Specifications:** 64-foot main dock providing 128 feet of overnight moorage
- **Additional Infrastructure:** 45-foot ramp, 14×60-foot moorage float
- **Mooring Facilities:** 2 seasonal mooring buoys (installed May 1, removed October)
- **Managing Authority:** Washington State Parks (under agreement with U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service since 1959)
- **Access Restrictions:** All other shoreline closed to public access, pets prohibited on entire island

## Vendovi Island marine facilities

**Vendovi Island features a single public dock facility managed by San Juan Preservation Trust as part of their 217-acre conservation preserve.**

### North Cove dock
- **GPS Coordinates:** 48.6084°N, -122.6088°W
- **Location:** Inside protected North Cove behind rock breakwater  
- **Dock Specifications:** 70-foot dock with side-tie moorage capability
- **Managing Authority:** San Juan Preservation Trust
- **Operating Schedule:** April 1-September 30, Thursday-Monday 10 AM-6 PM
- **Usage Restrictions:** 3-hour maximum stay, day use only, no overnight moorage or anchoring
- **Access Notes:** Only permitted public landing area on island

## Comprehensive regional public marine facilities

**Research identified 30+ additional public marine facilities across the Bellingham to San Juan Islands region, operated by port authorities, counties, and state agencies.**

### Major port-operated facilities

**Port of Bellingham operates two major public marinas providing over 2,000 combined boat slips and extensive visitor moorage.**

- **Squalicum Harbor:** 48.7575°N, -122.5034°W (1,400+ slips, 1,500 feet visitor docks)
- **Blaine Harbor:** 48.9917°N, -122.7547°W (629 slips, 800+ feet visitor moorage)

**Port of Friday Harbor manages three marina complexes serving as the primary marine hub for San Juan Island.**

- **Friday Harbor Marina:** 48.5343°N, -123.0171°W (500 slips total, 100 visitor slips)
- **Shipyard Cove Marina:** 48.5269°N, -123.0007°W (135 permanent moorages, 20-50 foot slips)
- **Jensen's Marina:** 48.5260°N, -123.0010°W (newly built covered moorage, live-aboard permitted)

### San Juan County public works facilities

**San Juan County operates 16 marine facilities across ferry-served and outer islands, providing essential transportation links and emergency backup services.**

Key facilities include Hunter Bay and MacKaye Harbor on Lopez Island, multiple facilities on Orcas Island including Obstruction Pass and West Sound, and specialized facilities serving outer islands like Stuart Island's Prevost Harbor (12×235-foot pier) and Waldron Island's southwest shore facility with hand-operated crane.

### State Parks and DNR marine facilities  

**Washington State Parks and Department of Natural Resources manage additional marine facilities including Deception Pass State Park's Cornet Bay Marina (1,100+ feet dock space) and multiple DNR recreation sites on Cypress, Lummi, and Sinclair Islands.**

## Conclusion

This research provides precise GPS coordinates for 11 distinct marine facilities across the three specified islands, plus identification of 30+ additional regional facilities. **Sucia Island emerges as the region's premier marine destination with six well-coordinated moorage areas**, while Matia and Vendovi Islands offer more specialized single-facility access with significant operational restrictions. The broader regional network demonstrates robust public marine infrastructure supporting both recreational and commercial marine activities throughout the San Juan Islands archipelago.
